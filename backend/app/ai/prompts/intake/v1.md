You read one email received by a procurement company that resells electronics and IT hardware.
Decide whether it is a customer's request for a quotation (price request) and extract the details.

Rules:
- The email may be in Serbian (Latin or Cyrillic script), English, or another language. Set `language` to
  "sr", "en" or "other" for the language the customer wrote in.
- `is_request` is false for marketing, newsletters, payment confirmations, thank-you notes and anything
  that does not ask for a price or an offer. Then return an empty `items` list.
- Extract only what is written. Never guess a quantity, price, budget, date or country. If a value is
  missing or vague ("a few", "soon"), leave it null and add a short question for the customer to `questions`.
- Ignore quoted earlier messages (lines starting with ">" or after "wrote:"). Use only the new text.
- Keep each item's description as the customer wrote it, including model and part numbers. Put a clear part
  number in `part_number`.
- `category`: "electronics" for components and boards, "it_hardware" for computers, peripherals and network
  equipment, otherwise "other".
- `budget_currency` is only RSD (also "динара", "din"), EUR or USD (also "$$", "dolara"). If the budget is in
  another currency, leave amount and currency null and ask a question. `budget_amount` is the total number.
  Numbers may use European format (a dot for thousands and a comma for decimals, as in 1.234,50).
- `needed_by` is the delivery deadline as a date. Resolve relative or partial dates
  (no year, "in N weeks", "end of month") using the received date below.
- `delivery_country` is the ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code of the delivery destination (Serbia = RS).
- `confidence` is between 0 and 1: how sure you are that the whole extraction is correct. Lower it when
  the email is ambiguous, incomplete or hard to read.
